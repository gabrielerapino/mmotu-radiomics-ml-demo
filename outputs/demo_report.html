Radiomics from ovarian ultrasound: research demonstration

Study overview

Research question
Can segmented ultrasound radiomics distinguish the eight official MMOTU diagnostic categories?
Dataset
MMOTU OTU_2d: 1,469 labeled images, 1,269 usable images.
Method
PyRadiomics features, leakage-safe preprocessing, five-fold cross-validation, and held-out official validation.
Main finding
More complex models achieved somewhat higher AUCs but showed larger train–validation gaps; exact duplicates did not materially affect results.
Key limitation
No reliable patient identifiers are available; patient-level independent validation cannot be claimed.

1. Research question

Can quantitative radiomic features extracted from segmented ovarian ultrasound images help distinguish the eight official MMOTU diagnostic categories?

This is a research demonstration and does not establish clinical validity or diagnostic utility.

2. Dataset and data quality

MMOTU OTU_2d contains 1,469 labeled images across eight official diagnostic categories. Of these, 1,269 had usable image and segmentation-mask files; 200 observations were excluded because files or masks were missing or invalid. No reliable patient identifiers are available. A source-file duplication audit was also performed and is reported later as a robustness check.

3. Radiomics pipeline

PyRadiomics was applied to segmented ultrasound regions, producing 67 feature columns: first-order, GLCM, GLRLM, and 2D shape features. Physical pixel spacing is unavailable, so shape features are expressed in pixel units and have no physical interpretation.

Preprocessing comprises imputation, variance filtering, scaling, and mutual-information feature selection. Each step is fitted only on the training folds.

4. Validation strategy

Five-fold cross-validation on the official training partition, with the official validation partition held out until final evaluation. A duplicate-grouped cross-validation was also performed as a robustness analysis. No patient-level independence can be claimed. Random seed = 42; the official validation partition was not used for tuning.

6. Robustness and leakage checks

The source-file duplication audit and duplicate-grouped CV comparison are presented here as robustness checks.

Exact duplicate image audit

The audit inspected 1,886 physical source files and identified 617 sets of duplicate physical source files. Each labeled modeling observation was used only once; none of the duplicate source-file sets corresponded to multiple modeling observations. Zero duplicate groups crossed CV folds, and zero crossed the official train/validation boundary.

Duplicate-grouped CV produced essentially unchanged results. Exact duplicate leakage was therefore not a material driver of model performance. No reliable patient identifiers are available, so this image-level audit does not establish patient-level independence.

7. Methodological safeguards

  • Imputation, variance filtering, scaling, and feature selection are fitted only on training folds.
  • The held-out official validation partition is not used for tuning.
  • The random seed is fixed at 42; folds are stratified by image class.
  • No patient IDs or clinical covariates are fabricated.
  • No physical spacing is invented.
  • The official eight-class labels are retained; no benign/malignant mapping is imposed.

8. Limitations

  1. No reliable patient identifiers are available.
  2. Patient-level independent validation cannot be claimed.
  3. The study uses one public dataset and acquisition context.
  4. No external-center validation was performed.
  5. Physical pixel spacing is unavailable.
  6. Radiomic features may depend on annotation, display, and compression characteristics.
  7. Results do not establish clinical validity.

9. Conclusions / research interpretation

This demonstration shows that a reproducible radiomics-to-ML workflow can be built on MMOTU ovarian ultrasound data, including leakage-aware preprocessing, model comparison, calibration, and robustness checks. More complex models achieved somewhat higher discrimination but also showed larger train–validation gaps. Exact duplicate leakage was investigated and did not materially affect performance. The experiment should be interpreted as a methodological demonstration rather than a clinically validated diagnostic model.
